Artist
TOGARBO

Togarbo creates oil paintings that inhabit the space between who we have been and who we are becoming.

Her work places figures within atmospheric and psychological landscapes — thresholds, corridors of light, windows, vast distances — where the environment itself becomes a map of interior states. Rather than resolving the tension between departure and arrival, her paintings hold it. What remains is the feeling of standing at the edge of something unnamed, suspended between memory and emergence.

Through restrained imagery and deliberate ambiguity, Togarbo renders transformation not as event but as condition — the sustained, quiet work of becoming.

Artist
BogaCA

BogaCA's work begins in observation and arrives somewhere stranger. From intimate wildlife portraits and expressive florals to quiet surrealist narratives where drawn creatures step off the page, her paintings reorganize the familiar through a child's internal logic — where scale shifts without explanation, objects carry feeling, and the world follows emotional rather than physical rules.

Her practice is rooted in an early encounter with drawing, formed in the quiet intervals after childhood lessons with an elderly neighbor. In those moments, drawing existed without hierarchy or expectation. Shortly before her passing, the mentor offered a final directive — to never stop drawing. This persists not as nostalgia, but as an underlying structure: a return to image-making as a way of thinking, remembering, and reconfiguring reality through instinct as much as observation.

For BogaCA, realism and surrealism are not opposites but coexisting conditions. The work does not escape reality; it reorganizes it.

05 of 15
The Red Window
BogaCA · Acrylic on Canvas · 20 × 16 in

Suspended between presence and absence, a solitary red window emerges from a field of shifting color. Neither attached to a structure nor anchored to a place, it becomes a symbol of memory, possibility, and passage. The warm light within is not painted — it is the canvas itself, left bare, glowing through thin layers of pigment. What illuminates the window is the very material the painting is made of. The work invites viewers to imagine what lies beyond the frame — and what remains unseen.

09 of 15
The Flower Blooms
BogaCA · Impasto Oil on Canvas · 24 × 36 in

The Flower Blooms stands as a celebration of painting itself. Rather than pursuing photographic realism, the artist allows each impasto stroke to remain visible, reminding the viewer that the image is built through countless deliberate gestures. The layered petals possess remarkable physical presence, while the surrounding foliage provides a restrained counterbalance that heightens the luminosity of the blossoms. The work speaks not only of botanical beauty but of perseverance, suggesting that growth is gradual, cumulative, and ultimately radiant.

BogaCA — Series
I drew a fish. It swam away. I made a friend. He stayed.

Drawn to Life is an ongoing series in which children's drawings step off the page and enter the world. Each painting begins with a crayon sketch — simple, imperfect, entirely a child's — and asks what happens when imagination refuses to stay contained.

The subtitles read like pages from a child's diary: simple enough for a child to say, emotionally resonant for an adult to feel.

10 of 15
I Drew a Fish
BogaCA · Oil on Canvas · 24 × 18 in
Drawn to Life Series — No. 1

A child's crayon drawing of a fish — visible in the open sketchbook below, rendered in crude blue scrawl — has come to life and is now standing in a puddle on the floor, dripping blue crayon-water from its mouth. The crayons are right there beside the sketchbook. The fish is bewildered. So, presumably, is the child. Some things you draw take on a life of their own.

11 of 15
He Stayed
BogaCA · Impasto Oil on Canvas · 24 × 18 in
Drawn to Life Series — No. 2

A simple drawing steps off the sketchbook page and into a warm, lived-in bedroom. The room is fully realized — teddy bear, dice, crayons, a lamp throwing amber light. He is flat, drawn, made of crayon lines. He stands in a space that was not made for him, and he looks completely happy to be there. He chose not to go back to the page. Without explanation, without ceremony — he stayed.

12 of 15
I Wanted a Yellow Car — Now I Have Two
BogaCA · Impasto Oil on Canvas · 24 × 18 in
Drawn to Life Series — No. 3

A child sits before something he once imagined on paper: a little yellow car, now standing in the world before him. But somewhere between drawing and reality, something unexpected happened. There are two.

In the third work from Bogaca's Drawn to Life series, imagination continues to cross the boundary into the physical world. Yet unlike the fish that swam away or the friend who stayed, this creation arrives with an excess the child never asked for — as though imagination has begun developing a sense of humor of its own.

I Wanted a Yellow Car — Now I Have Two is about the uncomplicated abundance of childhood dreaming: a time when wanting something could begin with a crayon, possibility had no sensible limits, and receiving more than you imagined seemed entirely reasonable. The child does not question the impossible. He simply sits and looks at his two yellow cars.

I Wanted a Yellow Car — Now I Have Two — Detail
13 of 15
I Wanted to Play — He Did Too
BogaCA · Impasto Oil on Canvas · 24 × 18 in
Drawn to Life Series — No. 4

It was already nighttime. The child wanted to go outside and play, but he couldn't. So he drew a little figure with the same sad expression he was feeling. Then the drawing came to life. Under a crescent moon, the little figure sits on a swing in the empty playground, surrounded by the things the child could only imagine from inside.

In the fourth painting from Bogaca's Drawn to Life series, a simple childhood disappointment becomes something magical. The drawing does not merely resemble what the child imagined — it goes where the child cannot and does what the child wishes he could do.

The crayons remain beside the abandoned sketchbook. The playground is no longer empty. He wanted to play. He did too.
